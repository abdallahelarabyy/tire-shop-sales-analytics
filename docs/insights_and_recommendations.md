# Insights & Recommendations

*Real POS data from a tire, battery and rim shop in Egypt. Money values are scaled for confidentiality
(see README); percentages, counts and patterns are real. Cost = POS last purchase price.*

## The shop in one paragraph

The shop sold **3.76M EGP** (3,189 units across 343 products) for **903K EGP profit
(24.0%)**. Between 1 Apr and 3 Nov 2026 it issued **2,036 invoices on 208 trading days - about
17K EGP and 10 invoices a day**, average ticket 1,698 EGP. Products are a
thin-margin business (about 12%); the **welding and rim-straightening services earn
56% of all profit**. It is an **evening shop**: 58% of invoices come after 18:00.

## Key findings

| # | Finding | Numbers |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Services are the profit engine** | 13.5% of revenue, **56% of profit**; 1,706 jobs at ~298 EGP profit each (vs ~238 per tire). |
| 2 | **Products earn thin margins** | Tires 11.7%, batteries 13.3%, rims 13.0%. |
| 3 | **Bigger, pricier tires earn the least** | By rim size: R12 23.2%, R13 13.9%, R14 13.3%, R15 13.7%, R16 11.7%, R17 8.9%, R18 7.9%, R19 9.3%, R20 9.1%. By price band: < 1.5K 14.2%, 1.5K-2K 14.7%, 2K-2.5K 10.4%, 2.5K-3K 9.6%, 3K-4K 11.1%, 4K+ 3.7%. |
| 4 | **Korean tires sell well at a low margin** | 546K revenue (2nd origin) at **8.8%**; Hankook 5.8%. China 14.7%, Vietnam 14.0%, Europe 14.7%. |
| 5 | **21 tires sold below last purchase price** | 7 small gaps (< 5 %), 9 medium (5-15 %), **5 large (> 15 %)** - worst: 235/45 R17 Hankook sold at 2,558 vs 3,832. |
| 6 | **Egyptian batteries earn ~2x the margin** | Chloride 17%-18%, Nesr 22% vs Varta 9.7%, ACDelco 7.8%. TD-70 = 75% of battery units. |
| 7 | **A long tail of stock** | 75 products sold only once; one size (185/65 R15) is stocked in 21 brands/origins. |
| 8 | **Evening business** | 58% of invoices after 18:00, peak 21:00; busiest slot Saturday 21:00. 82 after-midnight invoices. |
| 9 | **July peak, autumn slowdown** | Saturday and Monday are the best days, Sunday the weakest. |
| 10 | **Few invoices carry the revenue** | Invoices of 3K+ are 16% of invoices but **62% of revenue**; 58% are under 1,000 EGP (mostly service jobs). |
| 11 | **No customer data** | 98% walk-in cash; no repeat-customer analysis is possible. |

## Recommendations

| Priority | Recommendation | Why / expected effect |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Update selling prices the day a more expensive batch is entered**, and add a minimum-margin warning in the POS | Cost = last purchase price, so a stale price list turns into below-cost sales. |
| 2 | **Re-price large tires (R17+)** | Lifting R17+ from 8.7% to 11 % on the same volume adds profit worth 2.3% of their revenue. |
| 3 | **Review Korean / Japanese / Turkish tire pricing**, or offer the 14-15 % origins in the same size | Korean tires at 12 % instead of 8.8% would add 3.2% of their revenue in profit. |
| 4 | **Cross-sell during service visits** - a free tire / battery check with every welding or straightening job | Services bring the traffic and the profit; a few extra tire or battery sales a week add high-value tickets. |
| 5 | **Push Egyptian battery lines** when the customer has no brand preference | Roughly double the margin on the same TD-70 sale. |
| 6 | **Trim the long tail** - keep 3-5 brands per popular size across price points | Frees cash tied up in slow stock. |
| 7 | **Record a phone number on every invoice** | Enables repeat-customer analysis and reminders. |
| 8 | **Staff for 19:00-23:00**; review opening before 12:00 | Mornings bring only 3% of invoices. |

## Caveats

- Margins are **at last purchase price**: when supplier prices rise, past profit is understated and some sales look like losses.
- Item revenue and invoice revenue cover different periods (item report to 22 Nov, invoices to 3 Nov).
- 117 invoice dates were corrected because the POS kept the open shift's date - see `cleaning_report.md`.
